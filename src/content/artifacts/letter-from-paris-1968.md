Paris, the 2nd of June

Dear Madeleine,

Your mother will have told you not to worry and for once she is right. The
telephone works most days now, the bakery on our corner never closed, and the
only thing I have lost in all of this is sleep, which I would have lost
anyway.

What the radio calls disorder looks, from my window, more like a meeting that
will not end. The committee from Michel's plant has been sitting in the
assembly hall for three weeks. He comes home hoarse. He says they are past
arguing about wages now and have started arguing about everything else, which
he says is either the end of the argument or the beginning of a very long one.

They have taken the flag down at the prefecture and put it back up twice. The
second time, someone had sewn something small near the pole — a flower, red,
about the size of a thumbprint. Nobody knows if it is official. Nobody has
taken it down.

Kiss the children. If things are still calm in August we will come, though I
begin to suspect that whatever this is, it is not going to be finished by
August.

Your loving cousin,
Béa
